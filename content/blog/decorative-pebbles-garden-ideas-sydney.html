Decorative pebbles are the lowest-maintenance surface a Sydney garden can have: no mowing, no mulch top-ups, no watering, and they look as good in year five as day one. Here are seven ways we see them used well across Sydney — plus the coverage numbers so you can order the right amount first time.

1. White pebble pathways

Snow white pebbles against dark garden beds is the classic Sydney coastal look. Use a 20–30mm rounded stone at about 40mm deep over compacted road base, with steel or timber edging to keep the line crisp. The white bounces light into shaded side passages that never see sun.

2. Crushed white for modern courtyards

Where the rounded stone reads "coastal", crushed snow white reads "architectural". The angular chips lock together underfoot, so it suits high-traffic courtyards and the strips between pavers. Pairs cleanly with concrete, rendered walls and cactus or succulent planting.

3. Charcoal feature beds

Charcoal grey pebbles do for silver and green foliage what a dark frame does for a photograph. Westringia, lomandra, agave — anything with structure stands out against a dark ground layer. Charcoal also hides leaf litter far better than white, so it's the practical pick under natives and gums.

4. Lava rock around fire pits and BBQ areas

Charcoal lava and red lava pebbles bring texture you don't get from river stone — porous, matte, volcanic. The red especially warms up entertaining areas and works with corten steel and hardwood decking.

5. Dry creek beds for drainage lines

Got a soggy path where stormwater runs? Shape it into a shallow swale, line it, and dress it with mixed-size pebbles. It moves water in a downpour and looks deliberate the other 360 days of the year.

6. Pot toppers and balcony gardens

A 20kg bag of pebbles dresses a dozen large pots — hides the soil, slows evaporation, stops potting mix splashing onto tiles, and deters curious pets from digging. Most of our pebble range is available in 20kg bags as well as bulk, which suits apartment jobs.

7. No-mow verges and strips

The narrow strip between driveway and fence that's impossible to mow? Pebble it once, edge it properly, and it's done forever. Council verges vary by area, so check local rules before converting a nature strip.

How much do you need?

Pebbles are laid thinner than mulch — 40–50mm is plenty for full coverage. As a rule of thumb, one tonne covers roughly 10–12m² at 40mm, varying with stone size. Always lay over weed matting: it keeps stone from sinking into soil and stops weeds coming up through the profile. Our coverage calculator has per-product numbers.
